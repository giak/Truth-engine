# Analytic body

## Object
INV-119 tests the chain social origin -> selective education -> ENA/INSP/IGF access -> senior administrative and cross-sector career circulation -> network/conflict opportunity -> specific policy decision. The central discipline is to distinguish structural reproduction and access from coordination, corruption and capture.

## Social selection is large and measurable
Current official data show a steep social gradient before the high civil service. In 2024-25, children of cadres/higher intellectual professions represented more than half of students in CPGE, engineering formations and ENS, against strong under-representation of worker-origin students [FCT-001]. Among 2024 bacheliers, 14.1% of cadre-origin students entered CPGE versus 4.1% of worker-origin students [FCT-003]. At ages 25-44, bac+5 attainment was 40% for people from cadre/intermediate-dominant households versus 7% for worker/inactive households [FCT-004]. Long-run INSEE work confirms persistent elite-school selection [FCT-005,FCT-006].

This closes reproduction/concentration as a descriptive structural mechanism. It does not identify corruption or a coordinated elite project.

## ENA/INSP: strong homogeneity, not an immutable caste
The 2021 INSP prefiguration report describes ENA recruitment as doubly homogeneous socially and academically. In 2020, 70% of students had a father in an upper socio-professional category [FCT-007]. DGAFP gives an even sharper 2019 contrast: about 1% worker-father versus 73% higher-intellectual-father [FCT-010]. Yet the same prefiguration report reports nearly 33% higher-education scholarship recipients in the two latest promotions [FCT-008]. Talents creates a distinct access route [FCT-011,FCT-012], and the 2025 general-route cohort reached gender parity [FCT-013].

The correct inference is strong reproduction with some mobility and reform capacity, not zero permeability. A comparable post-reform parental-origin series remains missing.

## IGF as access and circulation architecture
The current IGF explicitly provides high-level mission exposure, encounters with senior officials and dense career connections [FCT-018]. Junior and inspector routes train candidates for leadership and now admit a broader set of A+ and, under conditions, non-civil-service profiles [FCT-016,FCT-017,FCT-019]. Since 2023 inspector positions are functional four-year jobs rather than the old permanent-corps allocation model [FCT-016].

Historical mobility is nonetheless material. Hervé Joly's Senate testimony on thirty cohorts/154 inspectors found nearly one third had resigned and many others were on availability in companies [FCT-023]; one small 2013 cohort saw four of five leave after the four-year tour [FCT-024]. Marie-Christine Lepetit supplied an institutional mechanism: the service is pyramidal with few natural long-term internal posts, so members disperse after the initial tour [FCT-025]. Senate secondary aggregates also report unusually high private-sector and banking exposure [FCT-026,FCT-027]. These figures establish a historically strong circulation pattern, but not the current post-2023 rate and not capture.

## Revolving doors: risk channel plus real controls
HATVP rendered 639 mobility opinions in 2024. More than 95% were compatible overall, but 74.3% carried reservations and 4.5% were incompatible [FCT-020]. For moves specifically toward the private sector, 76.4% were compatible with reservations and 8.5% incompatible [FCT-021]. In 2025 the authority still examined 641 mobility opinions [FCT-022]. The 2021 reform also requires independence/impartiality safeguards for relevant inspection services [FCT-015].

This establishes that revolving doors are recognized conflict-risk channels and that mitigation is institutionalized. Neither approval nor a reservation establishes the presence or absence of actual later influence; case-level compliance and decision rights remain necessary.

## Network is not coordination
Elite schools and IGF can create shared training, repeated interaction, prestige and access. Those are plausible mechanisms for homophily and opportunity concentration. The bounded searches did not identify authenticated evidence that common school/corps membership alone creates a shared political agenda or coordinated tasking. The corporate comparator also shows concentration can change: research summarized by Sciences Po reports the share of CAC 40 board members from the most elite schools fell from 36.36% in 2008 to 26.99% in 2017 as board composition diversified [FCT-028].

Thus network/access is supported; coordination is unresolved unless communications, joint strategy or repeated synchronized interventions are shown.

## Capture is a separate decision-level claim
No source in this bounded run closes the generic edge elite concentration -> revolving door -> captured policy. To establish capture requires a named actor and beneficiary, prior decision rights, a relevant private interest, post-move contact/intervention, an identifiable decision or text delta, and credible control of rival explanations. Upstream concentration raises the prior probability of conflicts and privileged access; it is not the downstream effect itself.

## Result
France shows strong social reproduction into elite education and high civil-service pathways, plus historically intense public-private circulation in the Inspection des finances. These mechanisms create concentrated access and networks. The 2021-2023 reforms materially alter recruitment and introduce/strengthen controls, so historical rates cannot be projected mechanically. The highest supported model is reproduction + selective access + career circulation + conflict-risk opportunity. Generic coordination, corruption and policy capture remain unclosed. INV-095 may use the supported selection/access mechanism, but not promote it into a coordinated pre-election elite system without additional evidence.
